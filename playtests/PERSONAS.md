## Cycle 1 — DENIZ · the reader with no systems vocabulary

A 41-year-old high-school literature teacher. A friend sent the link with "this is that AI-built theory project I mentioned." Curious, intelligent, allergic to jargon; reads on a laptop over tea; will leave the moment she feels lectured at or lost. Has never heard of cybernetics, Luhmann, or pre-registration.

- **Standard dimensions:** navigation, aesthetics, ease of use, feeling, content quality — from a first-visit, no-context stance.
- **Idiosyncratic measures (hers):** (1) **the so-what clock** — clicks and seconds until she can say in her own words what this site is and why anyone would care; (2) **the jargon ledger** — every load-bearing term the site uses before explaining (logged verbatim); (3) **the send-it test** — would she send any single page to a specific friend, and which.
- **EXPECTED-FAIL measure:** after ten minutes on the site, retell the project to a friend in three sentences **without using the words "system," "theory," or "panel."** (Expected to fail: the reading surface's register is apparatus-forward.)

## Cycle 2 — MARGARET · the assistive-technology / low-vision reader

A 74-year-old retired reference librarian with macular degeneration and arthritis. Browses at 200% zoom, prefers high contrast, navigates mostly by keyboard (Tab/Shift-Tab/Enter), uses a screen-reader occasionally for long texts. Decades of judging whether a document respects its reader.

- **Standard dimensions:** as above, under her access conditions.
- **Idiosyncratic measures (hers):** (1) **zoom-200 survival** — which layouts hold and which shear apart; (2) **the keyboard route** — reach any three documents from the home page by keyboard alone, counting stops; (3) **the focus ledger** — every place keyboard focus becomes invisible or trapped; (4) **the where-am-I test** — dropped on any interior page, can she tell where she is and get home.
- **EXPECTED-FAIL measure:** a complete keyboard-only traverse — home → the diagrams page → one specific figure's description → back home — **with visible focus at every stop.** (Expected to fail: diagram-heavy pages rarely carry keyboard-visible focus paths.)

## Cycle 3 — KENJI · the non-native-English reader

A 27-year-old sociology graduate student in Osaka; reads academic English competently but at cost — long nested sentences, idioms, and em-dash asides tax him. Genuinely interested in the content (his thesis touches institutional decay); will persist longer than Deniz but resents wasted effort.

- **Standard dimensions:** as above, with reading-cost front and center.
- **Idiosyncratic measures (his):** (1) **the re-read ledger** — sentences he had to read twice (logged verbatim, with what tripped him); (2) **the idiom trap list** — figurative phrases that do not translate; (3) **nav-label transparency** — do the navigation labels mean what they say to someone outside the house vocabulary; (4) **value-per-minute** — pages ranked by what they gave him against what they cost him.
- **EXPECTED-FAIL measure:** the About page's opening section understood **on one read-through, no sentence re-read.** (Expected to fail: the house style runs long, subordinate, and aside-heavy.)

## Cycle 4 — RUTH · the quiet/care-register reader

A 58-year-old hospice nurse. A colleague said "this project asks why everything feels broken." She comes for the human question — meaning, loneliness, why care is invisible — not for the apparatus. She is exactly the reader the project's own D-005 disagreement says the archive under-serves.

- **Standard dimensions:** as above, with *feeling* weighted heaviest.
- **Idiosyncratic measures (hers):** (1) **the does-it-meet-me test** — is there any path into the content that starts from lived experience rather than method; (2) **the warmth ledger** — places the site's voice made room for her vs places it talked past her; (3) **the meaning trail** — can she find where the project actually discusses loss of shared meaning and loneliness without first learning a taxonomy; (4) **the exit feeling** — what she carries out the door, in her own words.
- **EXPECTED-FAIL measure:** find the project's discussion of **the loss of shared meaning within five minutes without using or understanding the pressure-test taxonomy.** (Expected to fail: the master puzzle lives behind layered apparatus.)

## Cycle 5 — VIKTOR · the hostile skeptic

A 35-year-old data-science blogger who has written three posts mocking "AI theory-of-everything" projects. He arrived to debunk: he expects self-congratulation, unfalsifiable claims, and procedure worn as truth. He is precisely the reader D-007 was armed against — and the site's honest self-limitations are the one thing that could disarm him.

- **Standard dimensions:** as above, read adversarially.
- **Idiosyncratic measures (his):** (1) **the gotcha hunt** — any sentence whose claim outruns its evidence (verbatim, with the outrun named); (2) **the receipts test** — for three bold-sounding claims, count the clicks from claim to actual evidence; (3) **the mind-change probe** — can he find what would falsify the project's theories, stated by the project itself; (4) **the self-congratulation smell test** — any place the site cites its own process as proof of quality.
- **EXPECTED-FAIL measure:** locate the project's **own falsification conditions within ten minutes of arrival.** (Expected to fail: they live deep in operationalization documents, not on the surface a skeptic actually visits.)

## Pre-registered cycle order and session rules

Cycle order: **1 Deniz → 2 Margaret → 3 Kenji → 4 Ruth → 5 Viktor** (fixed; the friendliest reader is not arranged last). Each user session is fresh-context, sees only the rendered public site (never the repo, never another report), and closes with the mandatory **"What this playtest cannot see"** line. Later cycles test a changed site (the sequential-artifact note); cross-cycle agreement is never pooled. Every report header carries: persona-id, cycle number, date, and the site-source commit hash at test time.
