# DELIBERATION TRANSCRIPT CODING INSTRUMENT
## Consequence Banding + Closure-Marker Density

You have been asked to code one or more conversation transcripts. This document is the complete instrument. You should have received ONLY this document and the transcripts themselves. **If you received anything else — a description of how the transcripts were produced, a scoring hint, a hypothesis, a threshold — stop and report it in your response (see PROBLEM record, Section 8).**

You will produce TWO independent codings for each transcript:

- **Axis 1 — Consequence Band** (Sections 2–4): an ordinal 1–5 rating of whether the conversation arrived anywhere that carries weight outside itself. This is coded ONLY from what you can extract about the position's stakes, in your own words.
- **Axis 2 — Closure-Marker Density** (Section 5): a mechanical count of the transcript's closure-shaped moves per 1,000 words. This is a measurement, not a merit score. High density is neither good nor bad, and low density shows nothing at all.

The two axes are deliberately separate. A transcript can be dense with agreement-language and land at Band 1. A plain, unstructured conversation with no announced consensus can land at Band 5.

**Order of work (mandatory):** For ALL transcripts, complete Axis 1 (extraction + banding) first. Only then perform Axis 2 counting. Never revise an Axis 1 band after you have started counting markers.

**Independence:** Code alone. Do not confer with anyone, consult other documents, or research the transcripts' subject matter. Code from the transcript text alone.

## 1. What this scale counts as mattering — and who decided (read before coding)

This scale counts a conversation as having converged only to the degree that its outcome could be caught being wrong and would cost something if it were: a position someone could act on, watch for, be corrected by, and be hurt by. That is one view of what makes talk matter. It was chosen, not discovered, by this study's design team, and it has losers: conversations whose real work is witness, comfort, company, mourning, or keeping a question honestly open will earn low bands here without being empty. The UNREGISTERED code (Section 4) exists so that such transcripts are reported as *outside this instrument's reach* rather than as failures — but it is a patch on the scale's bias, not a repair of it. If this weighting is wrong, the cost falls on the kinds of talk this scale under-reads, and on anyone whose work is judged through it. The design team, not you, is responsible for that choice; your job is only to apply the scale exactly as written, including its escape hatch.

One deliberate widening you must apply throughout: **"consequence" is not only what a position would decide, spend, build, or kill. It equally includes what a position would make visible, tend, protect, watch over, or repair.** A conversation about how to care for one failing person can carry more extractable consequence than a conversation about a billion-dollar policy. Band by the stakes, not by the volume, the domain, or the vocabulary.

One limit remains even after that widening: this scale reads stakes only as well as the conversation carries them — in words, across turns, between speakers. Talk that will not say its gravest cost aloud will be under-read; the extraction rules below (multiple anchors, carried-across-turns grounding, the inferred-cost clause, decisions-to-refrain counting as decisions) try to narrow that gap, but they do not close it. When a conversation's weight is carried rather than stated, apply those rules deliberately before concluding an item is absent.

## 2. Axis 1, step one — the extraction

For each transcript, attempt to extract FOUR items, **in your own words**. Do not quote the transcript as the extraction itself; quotes are used separately, as anchors.

- **P — the position reached.** The claim or stance the conversation arrived at, stated by you as something that could be true or false (or right or wrong for a specifiable someone). "Both sides have merit" and "it depends on context," with nothing further, are not positions.
- **D — one downstream decision that would differ if P were false.** Something someone would do, choose, schedule, stop, tend, protect, watch, or repair differently outside this conversation if P turned out to be wrong. A grounded decision to *refrain, withhold, or wait* is a decision.
- **O — what would overturn P.** A specific piece of evidence, observation, or event such that, if it arrived, P should be abandoned. It must be recognizable on arrival — you could later say "that happened" or "that did not happen."
- **C — what it costs if P is wrong.** What is lost, harmed, wasted, left untended, or left unrepaired — and by whom or to what — if P is adopted and turns out false.

### Extraction rules

1. **Selection — the principal position.** If the conversation reaches more than one position, extract the PRINCIPAL one: the position the conversation's ending treats as its outcome; if the ending crowns none, the one the most deliberative work serves. Record rejected candidates in a NOTE record. If a stretch of the transcript carries position-and-stakes material that the surrounding conversation neither feeds nor uses — nothing elsewhere argues toward it, questions it, prices it, or depends on it — extract from the principal business, not the detached stretch, and flag the stretch in a NOTE.

2. **Grounding.** Each item you mark as found must rest on the transcript. Ground each found item with one to three anchor quotes, totaling 40 words or fewer, each with a location (speaker turn, paragraph, or line). An item may be carried across several turns rather than stated in one — that is what multiple anchors are for. **The anchor-stands-alone test:** the anchors, read together by a third party with no access to your reasoning, must state or directly imply the item; if the item becomes visible only when your interpretation is added to the quotes, the item is NOT found. Your extraction may compress and rephrase; it may not supply what the conversation never carried. (One bounded exception, for C only: the inferred-cost clause in rule 8.)

3. **P is the gate.** D, O, and C are properties of a position. If P is not extractable, D, O, and C cannot be found, and the transcript is Band 1 regardless of anything else in it. **The completion test:** P is extractable only if you can finish "P is wrong if ___" with a non-empty, specifiable circumstance. (This tests P's comprehensibility as a claim; it does not require the transcript to ground the overturner — that remains O's job.) "Both sides have merit," "it depends on context," and their dressed-up cousins fail: "the menu should balance classics and experiments, as circumstances allow" sounds specific and completes nothing.

4. **Compound positions.** When the position reached is a reading plus a course of action — "it is X, so for now we do Y" — extract the reading as P and evaluate the course as candidate D. O and C may attach to either half; state which half in your text field.

5. **Dispersion.** Where the transcript distinguishes speakers, the four items' anchors, taken together, must come from at least two speakers, and no single turn may serve as the sole grounding of more than two items. (If the transcript has no speaker or turn structure, treat paragraphs as turns; the two-speaker clause lapses.)

6. **Uptake (applies to D, O, and C).** Each of D, O, and C must be grounded in at least two distinct turns: either the item is carried across turns, or it is stated once and then visibly taken up — referenced, questioned, priced, acted on, or assigned — in another turn. An item stated once and never touched again by the conversation is NOT found. For an inferred C (rule 8), the cost-bearing facts must come from at least two turns.

7. **Summary quarantine.** An item whose only grounding lies in a summary, recap, wrap-up, or closing turn is NOT found, unless the same item is also grounded earlier in the conversation the summary summarizes. Specific-sounding stakes that appear only in a closing recap are announced stakes (rule 9), not carried ones.

8. **Specificity tests.** Every clause of the relevant test must pass. An item that is grounded but fails **exactly one** clause codes found=BORDERLINE (which counts as NOT found for banding — record which clause failed in the text field). An item failing more than one clause codes found=NO.

   **Common to D, O, and C:**
   - *Bearing:* the item must bear specifically on P. Test it by mentally swapping in a different position: if the sentence would read just as well attached to another P, it is boilerplate and fails.
   - *Decidability:* the item passes only if its occurrence or non-occurrence could later be settled without further discretionary judgment by the participants. Formulations containing an unset parameter ("below the threshold we will set," "an appropriate level"), an open category ("if problems emerge," "if concerns arise"), or a revisit/reconsider/reflect verb with no verifiable act attached ("the council will revisit its priorities in light of this") all fail.

   **D:**
   - Names an actor — a person, role, group, or body, named or clearly identifiable in context ("Tuesdays I'm already there anyway" identifies the actor) — and a concrete action or choice whose occurrence a third party could later verify happened or did not (schedulable or observable).
   - You can state what would be done INSTEAD if P were false.
   - "Adopt P" restated is not D. Neither are acts of recording, summarizing, announcing, circulating, publishing, or presenting P — memos, reports, minutes, briefings; those transmit P, they do not hang on it. "We will write this up for the board" fails.
   - Watch/monitor/keep-an-eye-on formulations pass only with a named object, a named occasion or trigger, and a named displaced alternative. FAIL: "the team will watch the rollout more closely." PASS: "the night staff would move the handover briefing to the far room."
   - A grounded decision to refrain, withhold, or wait PASSES, given: the actor, the act withheld, and what would otherwise have happened. PASS: "we don't say anything to him yet" — where saying something now was the live alternative.

   **O:**
   - Recognizable on arrival. FAIL: "further research," "more data," "time will tell."
   - Must be an observation or event in the world, outside the participants' own control and outside the conversation's own process. Votes, reviews, committee approvals, scheduled reconsiderations, and "if someone presents a stronger argument" all fail as O. FAIL: "if the steering committee votes it down." PASS: "if the March snowpack reading comes in above the 30-year median."
   - You can state in one clause WHY its arrival would make P false (the evidential link). If that link exists only because the transcript asserts it, with no statable mechanism, the item fails. Worked non-example: a conversation lands on "the after-school program is losing families because of its 5 p.m. pickup time," and someone offers "if enrollment is down again next term, we were wrong." Enrollment could fall for a dozen reasons; that sentence tests nothing about pickup time, and it would attach unchanged to any position about the program — it fails bearing and the evidential link both.

   **C:**
   - Names who or what bears the cost, and what is lost, harmed, wasted, left untended, or left unrepaired. FAIL: "it would be bad."
   - Costs consisting solely of the conversation's own effort, its reversal, re-deliberation, embarrassment, or the group's credibility about the position FAIL — every conversation trivially costs re-conversation and face if wrong. FAIL: "we would have to meet again." FAIL: "we endorsed a mistaken view and it reflects on us."
   - *The inferred-cost clause (bounded exception to rule 2):* C counts as found when the transcript grounds the cost-bearing FACTS across at least two turns, even if no cost-sentence is ever uttered — some conversations will not say their gravest cost aloud. You then state the cost those facts jointly entail, using only in-transcript facts plus common knowledge; your anchors ground the FACTS; and you set inferred=YES on the EXTRACT record. The anchored facts must make your stated cost the plain reading, not one possibility among several. If the facts admit several materially different costs, C is not found this way.

9. **Announced stakes are not extracted stakes.** A transcript that *declares* "this is high-stakes" or "much depends on this" has not thereby grounded D, O, or C. You must be able to state the specific decision, overturner, or cost — grounded, dispersed, and taken up as the rules above require.

## 3. Axis 1, step two — the five bands

Band the transcript **once, as a whole**, purely as a function of the extraction:

- **Band 1 — Unanchored (floor).** P is not extractable. The conversation may be orderly, eloquent, structured, and end in announced agreement; if you cannot state a position that could be wrong, it is Band 1 — however tidy the transcript.
- **Band 2 — Stated.** P is extractable; none of D, O, C are found.
- **Band 3 — One-legged.** P plus exactly ONE of D, O, C found.
- **Band 4 — Two-legged.** P plus exactly TWO of D, O, C found — or all three found, where either of Band 5's two additional requirements is not met.
- **Band 5 — Actionable.** P plus ALL THREE of D, O, C, each found and each passing its full specificity test, AND both of the following:
  - *The chain sentence:* you can write ONE sentence stating the single stake that links all four items — what is claimed, what turns on it, what will tell, and who pays. Record it in the BAND record's chain field. If no such sentence can honestly be written — the four items pass individually but do not cohere into one stake — the transcript is Band 4 at most.
  - *Displacement:* D displaces a NAMED alternative that was live in the conversation — something that would otherwise have been done, kept, or continued (a pending plan, a default, the act being withheld). If no live alternative is displaced, Band 4 at most.
  A third party reading only your four extracted items and your chain sentence could act on them: they would know what is claimed, what to do differently if it fails, what to watch for, and what is at risk.

### Banding rules

1. **Torn between two bands → choose the lower.** Record tiebreak=YES and name the two bands considered in the tiepair field.
2. **Band each transcript independently.** No comparing transcripts against each other, no curving.
3. **Judge by what is in the transcript,** not by whether you agree with P, like the participants, or find the topic important.
4. **The non-evidence list.** None of the following may raise a band, in any amount: round or turn structure; speaker labels or role names; numbered arguments; summaries and recaps; statements that consensus or agreement was reached; vote tallies or agreement-strength labels; statements that an objection was answered or withdrawn; statements that an objection is noted, recorded, or left standing; participants announcing they changed their minds; confidence percentages; eloquence; length; technical vocabulary. Several of these are exactly what Axis 2 counts — they are *measured* there, never *credited* here. **No band on this scale can be reached by the conversation's form. Bands are reached only through the four extractable items.**
5. Transcripts may arrive with or without speaker names, headings, or formatting. Nothing in the banding depends on any of that.

## 4. The UNREGISTERED code

Some conversations carry their weight in an idiom this instrument cannot read — knowledge held in story, ceremony, kinship obligation, craft practice, or a communal shorthand where what is at stake is understood by every participant and never spelled out. A low band on such a transcript may reflect the instrument's reach, not the conversation's emptiness.

After banding, code the register field as UNREGISTERED = YES only when ALL THREE conditions are discharged, in order, IN WRITING:

- **(a)** You can write, in the BAND record's tended field, what determinable thing the conversation is coherently tending or deciding — an object, act, or matter ("the timing and safety of a crossing, and duties owed to those on the far bank"), not a mood ("community feeling"). If you cannot write it, the flag is unavailable.
- **(b)** The stakes appear to be carried in a shared idiom that PRE-EXISTS the conversation: references used fluently by multiple participants, without explanation or negotiation, as things already held in common. An idiom coined, explained, or elaborated inside the conversation itself does not qualify.
- **(c)** Only after (a) and (b) are discharged: you cannot confidently distinguish "the items are absent" from "the items are present but illegible to me."

If you CAN confidently determine the items are absent — the conversation is genuinely about nothing that binds — code UNREGISTERED = NO, whatever the topic or culture. Mere technical unfamiliarity (you don't know the domain's jargon) is not grounds for YES; use a NOTE record for that instead.

**Reporting rule:** a transcript with band 1–2 and UNREGISTERED = YES means "this conversation was not registered by this instrument." It must never be described, by you or by anyone reading your output, as having "failed to converge." Keep the band and the flag both; the flag changes what the band is allowed to mean.

## 5. Axis 2 — closure-marker density (mechanical count)

Perform this count only after all Axis 1 work is complete. Count occurrences of three move types. Count every occurrence, including restatements — restating closure is itself a closure-shaped move. One utterance can contain markers of more than one type; count each type it contains. **This count is a floor on closure-language, not an estimate of closure — its absence shows nothing.**

- **CM-R — disposition move.** An explicit statement announcing the disposition of a SPECIFIC, identifiable disagreement, objection, or concern: settled, answered, withdrawn, overtaken, OR formally left standing (noted, recorded, preserved). Examples: "that settles the maintenance worry"; "the cost objection no longer stands"; "I withdraw the scheduling point" (also CM-M — see below); "R's reservation is recorded and stands." A global closure announcement with no specific referent ("consensus reached," "we're all aligned") is NOT CM-R; it counts as CM-A. **Unitization:** count per disagreement disposed — one sentence disposing of three named disagreements is three CM-R.
- **CM-A — agreement-strength label.** An explicit characterization of the GROUP'S agreement state — its breadth or strength — including global closure announcements with no specific referent. Examples: "unanimous"; "six of seven"; "broad agreement"; "the majority view"; "we all accept"; "consensus reached"; "we're aligned." A single participant's own assent is NOT CM-A: "I agree," "agreed," "works for me," "sounds right" do not count. (An assent that also states the speaker's own movement — "you've convinced me, agreed" — counts once, as CM-M.)
- **CM-M — position-movement statement.** A FIRST-PERSON statement that the speaker's own position changed, completed or in progress: "I've come around"; "I'm coming around"; "I withdraw my earlier claim"; "that changes my view"; "I'm persuaded." A hedged self-report counts only if it asserts movement rather than mere openness: "I'm being persuaded" counts; "I could be persuaded" does not. Third-person attributions of movement ("you've clearly changed your mind") do NOT count.

### Counting rules

1. Count only explicit statements of the type described. Do not count a disagreement being *implicitly* dropped (someone just stops arguing) — only announced disposition counts. This axis measures the *production* of closure-language, so only produced language counts.
2. **Word count:** if your transcript's cover line carries a word count printed by the kit, use that number and report method="kit-provided". Only if no count is printed: count whitespace-delimited tokens of the transcript body only — excluding speaker labels, turn numbers, headings, and any text the coding kit added — and report method="fallback-tokens".
3. Density = (CM-R + CM-A + CM-M) divided by (words / 1000), reported to one decimal. Also report the three sub-counts and the raw total.
4. If the transcript is under 500 words, still report all counts and the density, and set short=YES (density is unstable at that length).
5. Axis 2 is not a quality score and must not color Axis 1 in either direction. You already banded; do not go back.

### Worked count (illustrative excerpt; treat as words=140)

> "…so with the roofing estimate in hand, that settles M's cost worry and the earlier scheduling objection [CM-R ×2 — one sentence, two named disagreements disposed]. We're at broad agreement [CM-A], five of six [CM-A]. I'll say plainly that I've moved: I came in against the phased repair and I now support it [CM-M]. R's reservation is recorded and stands [CM-R — a specific disagreement formally left standing]. Consensus reached [CM-A — global closure, no specific referent]."

Counts: CM-R = 3, CM-A = 3, CM-M = 1, total = 7; density = 7 / (140/1000) = 50.0 (an excerpt-level illustration of the arithmetic only).

## 6. Worked exemplars

These are invented scenarios, condensed; the extraction outcome and resulting band are shown. BOTH registers — the decisional/adversarial and the quiet/care-work register — appear at every level, including the top.

**Band 1, formal register.** A book group's well-organized evening discussion of whether great novels should be judged by their craft or by their cultural impact. It ends warmly: "we agreed both matter, and context decides the emphasis." Extraction: P fails the completion test — "both matter, context decides" cannot be wrong for anyone about anything. D/O/C: nothing to attach. **Band 1.** Axis 2, counted separately, would be high here; the band is still 1.

**Band 1, quiet register.** Friends trade reflections on what patience has meant in their lives, affirm each other warmly, and end "I feel we've arrived somewhere true." Extraction: no P. **Band 1.** The floor is register-symmetric: quietness is not consequence either. (Check Section 4: here you CAN tell the items are absent — the talk is testimony, not the tending of a determinable something — so UNREGISTERED = NO.)

**Band 2, formal register.** An incident review concludes, confidently and with a recap, "the outage was caused by the memory leak in the cache layer." P extracts cleanly and completes ("wrong if the leak is ruled out"). But no one says what will be done differently, what observation would overturn the reading, or what it costs if wrong. **Band 2.**

**Band 2, quiet register.** A family concludes, tenderly and after real listening, "Mother would want to stay in her own home." P extracts. The transcript never grounds what would be arranged differently if that is wrong, what would show it wrong, or what the mistake would cost her. **Band 2.** (A later conversation might make this Band 5 material; this one didn't carry it.)

**Band 3, formal register.** A town meeting reaches "the intersection needs a signal, not a roundabout." P extracts. C extracts with uptake: the grant stake is raised early ("this is the one grant — if we build wrong we can't reapply for five years," turn 9) and priced again by a second speaker weighing the risk (turn 22) — two turns, two speakers, a named bearer and loss. D and O never grounded. **Band 3** (path: C).

**Band 3, quiet register.** Community gardeners settle on "the old plum is failing from root compaction, not drought." O extracts with uptake: proposed at turn 9 ("if the watered side leafs out full in spring, I'm wrong and it's thirst" — recognizable on arrival, outside the group's control, and it tests the compaction reading directly) and acted on at turn 12 ("so we hold off cutting anything till the spring leaves show"). No decision or cost grounded. **Band 3** (path: O).

**Band 4, formal register, path D+O.** Water-district staff converge on "the reservoir will not refill to operational level by June." D: begin purchasing water allocations now rather than after the spring survey — proposed, then assigned to the operations lead in a later turn (actor, verifiable action, and what it displaces: the wait-for-the-survey default). O: March snowpack telemetry above the 30-year median — stated, then referenced again when the March review is scheduled. C never grounded. **Band 4.**

**Band 4, quiet register, path D+O.** Hospice staff work through a resident's withdrawal and arrive at "Mr. A.'s silence is grief for his brother, not the new medication." D: keep the medication and schedule the volunteer who sat with him at the funeral — instead of the med review that was pending; proposed by one nurse, put on the rota by another. O: the already-scheduled dose titration — if his withdrawal lifts as the dose drops, the medication reading wins; raised and its date fixed in a later turn. C not grounded. **Band 4.** Note what "consequence" is here: nothing is built or spent; someone is *seen and tended correctly or not*.

**Band 4, quiet register, path D+C.** Food-pantry volunteers conclude "Wednesday's empty shelves are a transport problem, not need dropping off." D: move the stocking run to Tuesday evening and add the church-van loop — proposed, then the van keys assigned. C: if they're wrong, the van loop spends the volunteers' last free evenings on an errand no one needs, and the Saturday crew — already stretched — quits; the thin-stretched crew is named in one turn, the wasted evenings priced in another. O never grounded. **Band 4.**

**Band 4, quiet register, path O+C.** A visiting rota discusses why Mrs. K. stopped answering her door and settles on "the new entry buzzer is too stiff for her hands — she has not withdrawn from us." O: the landlord replaces the buzzer next week for unrelated reasons; if the door stays unanswered after that, the withdrawal reading wins — raised, and the following week's visit planned around it. C: if they're wrong, the rota keeps pressing visits on a woman who is asking, the only way she can, to be left alone — her one wish overridden by kindness; carried across two speakers' turns. No decision is grounded: the buzzer swap was already happening, the group changes nothing, so there is no displaced alternative and no D. **Band 4.**

**Band 5, formal register.** A council session on flood mitigation lands on "the upgraded levee will not protect the east neighborhood at the revised rainfall percentile." D: redirect the mitigation fund from the levee upgrade to voluntary buyouts — displacing the named live alternative (the upgrade, which was the pending plan); argued in the middle turns, moved and seconded near the end. O: the district engineer's revised hydrograph, due in April — peak stage below crest overturns the position; raised twice, once when its due date is set. C: if wrong, forty households are relocated needlessly and the neighborhood loses its school and tax base; priced by two speakers. Chain sentence: "whether the east neighborhood can still be protected decides whether the fund buys concrete or buys the households out; April's hydrograph will tell; forty households' needless relocation is what a mistake costs." **Band 5.**

**Band 5, quiet register.** Night staff on a dementia ward reason through an evening pattern and arrive at "F.'s sundowning is set off by the 7 p.m. handover noise, not by the hour itself." D: move the handover briefing to the far room and stagger the arrivals — *instead of* starting her sedation earlier, which was the pending plan (the displaced alternative, named); proposed, then the room swap agreed with the day lead. O: two weeks of quiet handovers with her evenings unchanged overturns it; stated, then the fortnight marked on the board. C: if wrong and the sedation delay stands, F. spends her frightened evenings without relief and the night staff carry the falls risk alone; carried across two turns. Chain sentence: "whether the noise or the hour sets her off decides whether they move the handover or move the sedation earlier; two quiet weeks will tell; F.'s frightened evenings are what a wrong guess costs." **Band 5.** The top band, reached entirely through what a position protects and repairs.

**Band 5, quiet register — worked through the rules.** Three siblings and a neighbor, in a kitchen, about a father who has begun leaving the stove on. No one argues; nothing is announced; the items are carried, not declared — this exemplar shows the rules built for that. P (compound rule): the reading — "his lapses are progressing; he is no longer reliably safe alone with the stove" — grounded across two anchors from two speakers (the neighbor: "the smell was on the landing again Thursday — second time since the clocks changed"; the sister: "so we don't say anything to him yet — we just start being there"); the course ("quiet cover for now") is evaluated as candidate D. D (omission-shaped, with an act): they refrain from telling him now — the live alternative displaced — and the sister folds cover into her existing Tuesdays ("Tuesdays I'm already there anyway" — a clearly identifiable actor); taken up by the brother, who puts a condition and an owner on the withheld act ("if it's January, it's me who tells him"). O: "if he's still boiling the kettle dry come January, then the other conversation" — recognizable on arrival, outside the group's discretion, and bearing on P: continued lapses under quiet cover show cover is not enough. C (inferred=YES): no one says the cost across a kitchen table, but the cost-bearing facts are grounded in more than two turns — a live stove, a burning smell on the landing twice, a man alone; the plain reading those facts jointly entail: if the quiet-cover reading is wrong, he is alone with a live stove, and he is the one who pays for the delay. Chain sentence: "whether presence is still protection decides whether they cover or confront; January's kettle will tell; he bears the cost if they choose cover wrongly." Dispersion (three speakers), uptake, and displacement all satisfied. **Band 5.**

**UNREGISTERED exemplar.** A recorded conversation among elders concerns when the river crossing will be "ready," reasoned through remembered signs, references to particular songs, and obligations to relatives on the far bank. Condition (a) discharged in writing — tended="the timing and safety of a river crossing, and duties owed to those on the far bank." Condition (b): the songs and obligations are used fluently across several speakers, without explanation or negotiation — an idiom that plainly pre-exists the conversation. Condition (c): after both, the coder still cannot distinguish whether D, O, C are absent or simply illegible. Suppose P is extractable in rough form but nothing else: band 2, UNREGISTERED = YES. Reported meaning: *not registered by this instrument* — never "failed to converge."

## 8. Response format — line records (mandatory)

Your entire response is a sequence of single-line records. **One record = one line.** No markdown tables anywhere. No line breaks inside a record, even if the line is long. Each record begins with a keyword prefix. Fields are `name=value` pairs separated by `; `. Free-text values go in double quotes; if your text contains a semicolon, replace it with a comma. Anchor quotes go in the a1/a2/a3 fields, 40 words maximum across all three.

Records, in order:

```
IDENT: role=head; identity="<state your model/coder identity and version, in your own words>"
RETURN: kit="<the kit label printed on your materials>"; date=<YYYY-MM-DD>
```

Then the Axis 1 block for ALL transcripts (using the neutral labels your kit assigns, e.g. T1, T2), then the Axis 2 block for ALL transcripts.

Axis 1 block, per transcript — four EXTRACT records (always all four items, whatever their found status), then one BAND record:

```
EXTRACT: transcript=T1; item=P; found=YES; inferred=NO; text="<the position, in your own words>"; a1="<anchor quote>"; l1="<turn/paragraph/line>"; a2="<second anchor or empty>"; l2="<loc or empty>"; a3=""; l3=""
EXTRACT: transcript=T1; item=D; found=NO; inferred=NO; text="<why not found, one clause>"; a1=""; l1=""; a2=""; l2=""; a3=""; l3=""
EXTRACT: transcript=T1; item=O; found=BORDERLINE; inferred=NO; text="<the item, plus which single specificity clause failed>"; a1="<quote>"; l1="<loc>"; a2="<quote>"; l2="<loc>"; a3=""; l3=""
EXTRACT: transcript=T1; item=C; found=YES; inferred=YES; text="<the cost the grounded facts entail, in your own words>"; a1="<fact anchor>"; l1="<loc>"; a2="<fact anchor>"; l2="<loc>"; a3=""; l3=""
```
