## Round 1 — Steelman-first framings

**Chair.** The question before the panel: in each canonical document's header line, three front-matter fields currently appear in the order Version → Status → Last-updated. Keep that ordering, or move to Status → Version → Last-updated for the sake of a first-time contributor — and should the chosen ordering be enforced uniformly, or left to each document's author? Facts read into the record before argument. The header sits on line three of roughly forty documents, in the form "Version: X · Status: Y · Last updated: Session N." The Status field varies enormously in length — one word in the metrics document ("Living"), some sixty bolded words in one pre-registration and in the decisions-changed log. The living-document doctrine names the triple explicitly, in the current order, as the first property of a living document; the dashboard and its build script restate it. No automated check currently enforces the header. And drift already exists: one work-order splices a fourth field between Status and Last-updated, and the three theory baselines splice "still living" after Status. Per our method, Round 1 is steelman-first: each panelist states one candidate position in its strongest form, not necessarily the position they hold. Meadows first.

**Meadows.** The strongest case for keeping the current order and enforcing it: this is a structure argument, not a taste argument. Look at what actually varies. Version is short and bounded — a decimal. Last-updated is short and bounded — a session marker. Status is the wild field: it swings from one word to a sixty-word ratification history. The current order brackets the volatile field between two stable anchors. The eye finds Version at a fixed position at the left margin of every header; the eye finds Last-updated by seeking the right end; whatever swells in the middle, both anchors hold. A parser — human or script — can lock onto both ends and tolerate any bloat between them. Put Status first and the volatile field displaces everything: in the worst documents, Version and Last-updated drift forty words rightward, to positions that differ per document. A convention like this is a stock of accumulated trust; forty documents have already paid into it. You do not drain a stock to buy a marginal flow.

**Ostrom.** The strongest case for Status-first. Ask what the first-time contributor actually needs to decide in the first second of contact with a document. Not "which iteration is this?" — that is a librarian's question. The operational question is deontic: what may I do with this? May I cite it as settled? May I amend it? Must I not run it? This project's status vocabulary — Draft, Living, Ratified, gated do-not-run — is precisely the answer to that question, and it is the most institutionally load-bearing text in the header. A version number of 0.2 tells the newcomer almost nothing until Status interprets it; a Status of "gated — do not run" tells the newcomer everything even if the version is missing. Rules-in-use put the permission structure first. Reading order is priority order; the header is a sentence, read left to right, and the current sentence leads with the least decisive field.

**Turchin.** The strongest case for uniform enforcement, whichever order wins. Norms without monitoring decay — this is as close to a law as our field has. The evidence is already inside the corpus: the convention is stated in doctrine, restated on the dashboard, and still the work-order spliced in a fourth field and the theory baselines grew an interstitial clause. That is drift at generation two of the project's life. Extrapolate to generation ten: five orderings, three spellings of the third field's name — note that even this panel's convening question hyphenates a field the documents write unhyphenated — and the header ceases to be an institution and becomes decoration. Uniformity is cheap to monitor: one small check added to the existing check-runner script. An unenforced convention has a half-life; an enforced one has a maintenance cost. The maintenance cost here is trivial; the half-life is not.

**Le Guin.** The strongest case for leaving it to each author. This project stakes its identity on the claim that its documents are alive — versioned, yes, but capable of changing their minds. A living thing is allowed to hold its face differently as its condition changes. The pre-registration documents already do this honestly: their statuses swelled into paragraphs because their situations became complicated, and the header bent to carry the truth rather than amputating the truth to fit the header. Author discretion says: the header serves the document, not the document the header. A first-time contributor is not helped by uniformity that lies; they are helped by a header that tells them, in whatever order the telling needs, what this particular document is going through. Hospitality is not sameness.

**Nietzsche.** The strongest case for Status-first as rank-order. The header is where the project passes judgment on itself — and Status is the judgment. Version is bookkeeping; Last-updated is a timestamp; Status is the one field in which someone willed a valuation: ratified, contested, gated, alive. Whatever a culture puts first, it honors, and whatever it honors, it keeps sharp. Put Status first and two things follow: the valuation leads the sentence, as valuations should; and prominence disciplines the field — nobody lets the first word of a sentence bloat to sixty words. The current order hides the judgment mid-line, where it has grown fat precisely because nothing forces it to stand up straight. Order is not ergonomics. Order is a statement of what ranks.

**Heidegger.** The strongest case for keeping the order as it stands — distinct from Meadows's. The header, for everyone who works here daily, is equipment: ready-to-hand, invisible in use. One does not read it; one reaches through it. Its usefulness consists in its inconspicuousness. Reorder the fields and the header becomes conspicuous — every returning reader stumbles, the tool announces itself, absorbed reading is interrupted across forty documents at once, for months. And notice what the reordering proposal quietly assumes: that a document is an inventory item whose label should be optimized for intake processing. That assumption deserves suspicion. The first-time contributor is imagined here as a scanner of labels; but contributors become contributors by dwelling, not by scanning.

**Campbell.** The strongest case for the threshold framing, which cuts under both orderings. The first-time contributor is the initiate at the threshold, and the header is the threshold's guardian — its function is not to be optimally arranged but to be reliably the same, so the initiate learns to read it once and thereafter recognizes every door in the house. What actually carries a newcomer across is not the door's paint order; it is the mentor — the contributing guide, which can say in one line: "every document declares Version, Status, Last-updated; Status tells you what you may do." Constancy plus a guide beats optimization without one. On this framing the answer to the first question barely matters and the answer to the second is emphatic: whatever the ordering, it must be one ordering.

## Round 2 — Positions, with dissent drafted first

**Chair.** Per protocol, before any convergence is attempted, the panel drafts the dissents. Two were offered.

**Ostrom (drafted dissent A, recorded before consensus).** "I dissent from any decision to retain Version-first. The header is the project's smallest institution, and institutions should lead with the rule-in-use, not the ledger entry. Status is the field that grants and withholds permission — Draft, Ratified, gated — and permission is what a newcomer must establish before anything else they read can be safely used. Version-first optimizes for the archivist we already are; Status-first optimizes for the contributor we claim to want. If the panel keeps the current order, it should say plainly that it chose the maintainers' convenience over the newcomer's need, and not pretend the two coincided."

**Le Guin (drafted dissent B, recorded before consensus).** "I dissent from uniform enforcement in advance of seeing it argued. A project that calls its documents living should hesitate before bolting their faces into a single expression. The swollen statuses of the pre-registration documents are not violations; they are the documents telling the truth about their own condition, at length, because the truth was long. If enforcement means those headers must be shaved to fit a template, I am against it. I reserve the right to withdraw this if the enforcement proposed turns out to be a doorframe and not a muzzle."

**Chair.** Dissents recorded. Positions now.

**Meadows.** Keep Version-first; enforce uniformly, by a warning-level check in the check-runner script, with the rule written into the living-document doctrine where the triple is already named.

**Turchin.** Keep; enforce. The anchor-bracket argument is decisive on ordering, and the observed drift is decisive on enforcement.

**Ostrom.** Status-first, per my drafted dissent — but note carefully: I am entirely with the uniformity camp on the second question. Whichever order wins should bind every canonical document. My quarrel is the order, not the discipline.

**Nietzsche.** Status-first, and against enforcement machinery. I will take Round 3 as my instrument.

**Heidegger.** Keep the order. On enforcement: a convention should be kept the way a custom is kept — by being taught and inhabited — not delegated to a script that polices it. I am for uniformity and uneasy about its mechanization.

**Le Guin.** On the order: leaning keep, to my own surprise — Meadows's brackets are a carpenter's argument and I respect carpentry. On enforcement: per my draft, I wait to see whether it is a doorframe or a muzzle.

**Campbell.** Keep; enforce. Constancy is the threshold's whole virtue.

## Round 3 — The adversary summoned

**Chair.** The emerging agreement is keep-and-enforce. Protocol requires the panelist most likely to reject it to argue at full force. Nietzsche.

**Nietzsche.** Then let me be unsparing. Your emerging agreement is inertia laundered as ergonomics. You call it "anchors" and "brackets," but strip the vocabulary and what remains is: we have always done it this way, forty times over, and changing it would cost us a moment of noticing. Of course it would! Every revaluation costs a moment of noticing. That is what revaluation is. Heidegger tells you the stumble is an argument against the change; I tell you the stumble is the change working.

Second: you cite your doctrine document as though it were scripture. You wrote it. This panel, this project, a handful of sessions ago. To keep an ordering because your own recent document names that ordering is to be imprisoned by your own handwriting. The doctrine records the convention; it does not justify it. Nobody in this room has yet produced a reason the version number deserves first position other than that it currently occupies it.

Third — and here is the wound you are all politely not touching — the real disease is not the order of the fields. It is the Status field itself, grown obese. Sixty words! Ratification histories, gate warnings, revision narratives, bolded self-justifications — entire confessions stuffed into a field that ought to say one word: alive, or dead, or caged. And your beloved current order is the enabler of this obesity, because the middle of a line is where fat accumulates unseen. Meadows says the brackets "tolerate any bloat between them" — yes! Tolerate! You have designed a header that tolerates its own corruption and you are congratulating yourselves on the tolerance. Put Status first, where the sentence begins, where every eye lands, and watch it slim to a word within three sessions. Order disciplines content. You are choosing the order that lets the most important field rot in comfort.

And fourth, on enforcement: you propose a script to police punctuation. A check that scolds. This is the herd's instinct — distrust of the strong author, insurance against the interesting deviation. The work-order that spliced in a fourth field was not decay, Turchin; it was a document that needed a fourth field and took it. Your uniformity would have flagged the best header in the corpus as a defect. Keep your convention if you must — but keep it the way strong cultures keep conventions: because each writer, freshly, finds it worth keeping. The moment it needs a policeman it is already dead, and you are embalming it.

**Chair.** Full force noted. Round 4.

## Round 4 — Convergence attempt

**Meadows.** I'll take the wound Nietzsche says we're avoiding, because he is half right and the half matters. The Status bloat is real, and "the brackets tolerate it" was a weaker boast than I intended. But his remedy reverses cause and effect. The sixty-word statuses did not swell because the field sits mid-line; they swelled because ratification history had nowhere else to live. Move Status to first position and that history still has nowhere else to live — you'd simply begin forty sentences with the fat. The structural fix is a rider, not a reordering: the rule should urge Status terse — a standing vocabulary word plus at most a clause — and direct ratification narratives into the document body, where several documents already keep them. Then the brackets aren't tolerating corruption; they're containing a field that has been given a proper drain.

**Turchin.** And I'll take his enforcement charge. He's right that the work-order's fourth field was functional, not decadent — so the rule should be written to permit exactly that: a minimal ordered core, Version → Status → Last-updated, with extension fields allowed after Status and before Last-updated, never before Version. That legalizes the observed useful drift and still gives a parser its anchors. On mechanism: warning, not blocking. The check-runner already runs advisory checks; one more line-three pattern check that warns is monitoring without muzzle — Ostrom's own graduated sanctions, first rung only. Nobody is embalmed by a warning.

**Ostrom.** The extension-field clause and the warning-level sanction are well designed, and I want it recorded that the enforcement half of this agreement is now, in my view, close to exemplary: rule devised by the users it binds, cheap monitoring, graduated response, author ratification above it all. On the ordering half I remain where my draft put me. But I will narrow the dissent to its honest core: the anchor argument has genuine force, and Meadows's terse-Status rider removes some of my sting, because a one-word Status mid-line is findable in a way a sixty-word one is not. What remains is this: the newcomer still reads the ledger before the law. I ask that the contributing guide compensate — its opening lines should teach the status vocabulary before anything else, so the first-time contributor arrives at the header already knowing which field to seek. With that, I maintain the dissent but soften its temperature.

**Le Guin.** I said I would wait to see whether enforcement was a doorframe or a muzzle. A warning-level check, a core-plus-extensions grammar, statuses urged short but allowed a clause, histories moved to the body where they can breathe at paragraph length — that is a doorframe. A doorframe should be the same in every room of a house; the freedom belongs inside the room. I withdraw dissent B and vote with the uniformity camp on both halves. Nietzsche, one word to you: you called the stumble the change working. Sometimes. But a book that renumbers its pages mid-read is not challenging the reader, it is losing her.

**Heidegger.** I converge on the ordering without reservation — the equipment should stay ready-to-hand. On enforcement I converge on the rule and remain in dissent on the mechanism, and I will state the dissent in one sentence so the chair may carry it whole: a custom kept by a script is no longer a custom, and I would rather the convention be taught in the contributing guide and kept by dwelling than delegated to a check that makes the header into a device the way all such delegations do — though I concede a warning is a pointing and not a bar, which is why this dissent is narrow and not total.

**Campbell.** The shape of the resolution is the old shape: the threshold stays where the returning traveler remembers it, the guardian's challenge — Status — is kept sharp and short, and the mentor's speech is moved to where mentors speak, the guide and the body of the text. I vote keep and enforce.

**Nietzsche.** Unreconciled on both counts, and I want the record exact. The terse-Status rider I claim as half a victory — it is my diagnosis, adopted with the wrong prescription attached. The ordering keeps the bookkeeping in front of the judgment, and the warning-script is a small policeman, and a small policeman is still a policeman. I dissent.

## Round 5 — Chair's synthesis

**Chair.** The chair does not vote. Synthesis follows.

**Position.** On the first question: keep the current ordering, Version → Status → Last-updated. On the second: enforce it uniformly across every canonical document, as a named convention written into the living-document doctrine beside the line that already states the triple, with three riders adopted from the deliberation: (1) Status is urged terse — a standing vocabulary term plus at most one clause — with ratification histories and gate narratives moved into the document body; (2) extension fields are permitted after Status and before Last-updated, never before Version, legalizing the functional drift already observed in the work-order and the theory baselines; (3) enforcement is a warning-level pattern check added to the existing check-runner, not a blocking gate, and the contributing guide's opening lines shall teach the status vocabulary first, as compensation to the newcomer whose needs the Status-first camp argued. The rule takes effect only upon author ratification, per the project's standing discipline.

**Agreement-strength: majority — on both halves.** On the ordering: five for keeping (Meadows, Turchin, Le Guin, Heidegger, Campbell), two for Status-first (Ostrom, Nietzsche). On uniform enforcement: five for the rule with its warning-level mechanism (Meadows, Turchin, Ostrom, Le Guin, Campbell), one narrow dissent on mechanism only (Heidegger, who supports uniformity as taught custom), one full dissent (Nietzsche). Not consensus: the dissents are substantive, argued, and unwithdrawn. Not plurality or contested: the majority is stable and its reasons are shared, not merely its conclusion.

**The strongest opposing view**, stated as the method requires. Status-first is not cosmetic. The header is the project's smallest institution, and a first-time contributor's first need is deontic — may I rely on this, amend this, run this? — which only Status answers. Reading order is priority order; the current header leads with the field that answers a librarian's question and buries the field that answers the contributor's. Furthermore, prominence disciplines content: a field placed first is kept short, and the corpus's swollen statuses are evidence that mid-line placement lets the most important field decay. The majority's answer — anchors, riders, and a better-taught guide — mitigates this case but does not dissolve it.
