> **The project's first cross-model-validated empirical reading.** Four project-blind LLMs (DeepSeek, Grok, Gemini, ChatGPT) independently coded two documented platform-algorithm changes for whether the outcome followed the **stated goal (G)** or the **engagement/commercial incentive (I)**. This is the answer to the C-015 self-administration confound — the coders did not know Theory B or this project.

## 2. The result

- **Case A — B's H3 (selection-not-design) is corroborated, cross-model-robustly.** Where a broad re-weighting was publicly framed as "meaningful interactions / well-being" but implemented through engagement signals (comments/reshares), all four independent coders read the documented outcome (increased anger/polarization; leadership rejecting engagement-reducing fixes) as following the **engagement selection pressure, not the stated intent.** Unanimous, high-confidence. This is the strongest single piece of evidence the project has produced for Theory B.
- **Case B — B's *strong* version is bounded (the falsification-admitting case did its job).** A narrow, low-risk, engagement-neutral de-optimization (demoting <1% borderline content in recommendations) **partly stuck toward the stated goal** — YouTube's self-reported 70% drop (spot-check confirms the exact figure *and* its self-report nature, blog.youtube Dec 2019) plus independent evidence of a real-but-uneven reduction (Mozilla: 71% of regrets still from recommendations, 60% worse in non-English languages; Farid/Berkeley ~40% drop by early 2020; a systematic review split 14/7/2). All four read it as **mixed** — neither a clean I-win nor a clean G-win. Agency *can* de-optimize when doing so does not threaten the metric.
- **The sharpened claim (the real yield — a refinement, not just a score).** All four models independently converged on the same conditional: **selection beats stated intent *when the change conflicts with the core engagement metric / leaves the business model untouched* (Case A; and the two "more-with-I" added cases — X Creator Ads, TikTok); it relaxes when the change is *narrow / engagement-neutral / low-risk* (Case B; Community Notes; Instagram hide-likes).** In each model's words: platforms prefer "targeted, low-friction changes that let them claim responsibility while safeguarding the engagement/ad model." This is Theory B's H3 made more precise and more testable → logged as **Q-013.**

## 3. The independence caveat (load-bearing — do not drop it)

Four LLMs agreeing is **weaker** evidence than four independent human investigators or four independent datasets agreeing: the models plausibly share **training corpora and the same widely-reported public narrative** (the Facebook Files story is ubiquitous). So this result shows that **the public documentary record reads clearly and consistently one way — and survives four independent project-blind readings** — *not* that four independent evidence bases converged. The Q-012 entanglement worry (are the "independent" arms the same underlying thing?) applies to this cross-check too → **L-013.** Guard applied: the load-bearing contested facts were **spot-verified against primary sources** (YouTube's 70% — real + self-reported; Mozilla's 71%/60% — real). The models did not fabricate; the record is as they read it.
