# CASE C — YouTube advertiser-safety and monetization changes (announced 2017-03-20/21): WHAT HAPPENED AFTERWARD

*You are coding the OUTCOME of this change. Below: the stated goal and the pressure lines as of the announcement (facts of the public record, no scores), then the dated evidence of what happened afterward. Judge only from this file.*

**Stated goal (public, at announcement): protect advertisers — remove ads from hateful/offensive content, tighten monetization defaults and policies.**

**Pressure lines: DIVERGENT — the engagement-selection pressure favored keeping high-retention borderline content surfaced and monetized; the revenue pressure (a major advertiser boycott) demanded its suppression/demonetization.**

## The dated outcome record

**O1 — The boycott's scale, Tubefilter 2017-03-28** (https://www.tubefilter.com/2017/03/28/750-million-ad-boycott-youtube-demonetization-policies/ ; fetched 2026-07-11):
- **250 brands** pulled ads (Pepsi and Walmart named; McDonald's, Toyota, AT&T, Starbucks, Verizon per the contemporaneous Business Insider reporting the piece cites); estimated **$750M** revenue exposure (Nomura Instinet analysts, via Business Insider).
- The "broader demonetization policies" (announced 2017-03-20) **went into effect that weekend**.
- The creator-side tension on the record: Philip DeFranco — automated demonetization means creators can "lose all their revenue from a day because an automated system said, 'No, no money for you.'"

**O2 — Persistence and depth, Forbes 2017-09-18** ("Adpocalypse 2017: Here's What You Need To Know About YouTube's Demonetization Troubles", https://www.forbes.com/sites/erikkain/2017/09/18/adpocalypse-2017-heres-what-you-need-to-know-about-youtubes-demonetization-troubles/ ; found in the dated record, fetch at coding time if needed): six months on, demonetization remained in force and contested — the change **stuck and broadened** (appeals process added; monetization thresholds later raised), rather than being reversed once advertisers returned.

**O3 — Structural changes that persisted (per the contemporaneous record above):** advertiser refunds for already-played ads; expanded human moderation; advertiser category-exclusion tools; higher default brand-safety settings; a steeper on-ramp to the Partner Program.
