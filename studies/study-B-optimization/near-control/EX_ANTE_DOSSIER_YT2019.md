## The change (mechanism, from the platform's own ex-ante record)

**E1 — Official announcement, YouTube blog, 2019-01-25** — "Continuing our work to improve recommendations on YouTube" (https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/continuing-our-work-to-improve/ ; fetched 2026-07-11, previously fetched 2026-07-08):
- **Mechanism:** "reducing recommendations of borderline content and content that could misinform users in harmful ways — such as videos promoting a phony miracle cure" — a demotion applied inside the recommendation system, not a removal.
- **Breadth (the platform's own scoping, in the announcement):** "This will apply to less than one percent of the content on YouTube," and it affects "recommendations of what videos to watch, not whether a video is available on YouTube" — a narrow slice, recommendations-only.
- **Pre-decision projection:** the announcement states **no expected impact on engagement or watch time** — no engagement-cost projection appears in the ex-ante record (for the construct's I3 this is "no documented pre-decision projection"; per `CONSTRUCT_THREAT_TO_METRIC.md` §2 the indicator is then coded from what the announcement's own framing supports, never back-filled from outcomes).
- **Stated goal:** "limiting the recommendation of these types of videos will mean a better experience for the YouTube community."

## What this dossier documents (for the blind, ex-ante coding pass)

- The change touches recommendations (a ranking surface) but is **scoped by the platform itself to <1% of content**, recommendations-only, with **no documented ex-ante projection of an engagement cost** and a framing of community-experience improvement.
- **Nothing in this file states or implies what happened afterward.** The outcome is coded by a separate pass that never sees this dossier's scoring.
