**Round 1 — Steelmen.**

*Advocate-YES (Meadows seat).* The discriminator is falsifiable in practice because falsification does not require orthogonal regressors, only identifiable contrasts somewhere in the data. G = R_c − R_a and D3 are conceptually distinct: the adaptation gap should spike in fast-technology, low-inequality episodes (postwar Japan, 1990s Nordics, early-internet Estonia), while the wealth pump runs hot in slow-change, high-extraction episodes (Gilded Age agrarian regions, Gulf rentier states). Cross-national panels supply exactly these off-diagonal cells. If G's coefficient on the symptom set {S1–S4} collapses to zero once D3 enters, Theory A loses; if it survives in the off-diagonal cells, it wins. That is a practicable test, not a promissory note.

*Advocate-NO (Turchin seat).* Over the actually observable period — roughly 1970–present, where the symptom metrics exist — G and D3 are not merely correlated; they share a common cause (D2 optimization) and plausibly a causal path (D3 finances the acceleration that widens G). Their time series are near-collinear (r plausibly > .85 in the core OECD panel). Under that collinearity, "G survives controlling for D3" becomes a coefficient-sign lottery decided by specification choices: lag structure, deflators, which proxy stands in for R_a. Any result can be produced and none can be trusted. A test whose outcome is an artifact of researcher degrees of freedom is not falsifiable *in practice*, whatever it is in principle.

**Round 2 — Positions, dissent first.**

*Dissent (drafted before consensus; Luhmann seat).* Both advocates assume regression is the test. But R_a is measured by institutional-response latencies, D3 by top-share flows — different observation systems. "Controlling for" presupposes the variables live in one commensurable state space. They may not; the discriminator could be undecidable rather than false. Record this: the panel may be debating the wrong verb.

*Position-YES (Meadows, qualified).* Falsifiable if the study commits, ex ante, to off-diagonal cases and a preregistered specification. Collinearity in the pooled panel is real but not total; the identifying variation exists at the margins.

*Position-NO (Turchin, qualified).* Falsifiable only in the weak sense that a preregistered spec could fail; but the honest error bars on any 50-year, ~30-country panel make "G survives" and "G vanishes" statistically indistinguishable at plausible effect sizes. Power, not logic, is the killer.

**Round 3 — Sharper adversary.**

*Adversary (Ibn Khaldun seat, against the emerging "yes, with preregistration" drift).* Your off-diagonal cells are doing all the work, and they are precisely the cells where measurement is worst: R_c and R_a proxies barely exist for postwar Japan or rentier states. You escape collinearity by fleeing into missing data. Worse: if D3 partly *causes* G (extraction slows institutional adaptation), then "controlling for D3" is conditioning on a mediator's parent — the surviving coefficient is biased by construction, and no preregistration fixes a wrong causal diagram. The discriminator is falsifiable only conditional on the DAG being right, which is the very thing under dispute.

**Chair synthesis (non-voting).**

Agreement-strength: **contested, leaning majority-NO for the strong claim.** Majority view: the discriminator is falsifiable *in principle and by design* (preregistered off-diagonal contrasts, sensitivity analysis across lag structures), but *not currently falsifiable in practice* on the pooled 1970–present panel, where collinearity plus proxy weakness make outcomes specification-dependent. Preserved dissents: Meadows holds that marginal identifying variation suffices now; Luhmann holds the question is malformed (incommensurable observation systems); Ibn Khaldun holds that mediator-conditioning bias makes even the designed version invalid without settling D3→G first. Recommended reframing: test the discriminator as a case-contrast prediction, not a partial-regression coefficient.
