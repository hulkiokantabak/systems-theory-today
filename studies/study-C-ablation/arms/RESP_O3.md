**Round 1 — steelmen.**

**Turchin (strongest YES — keep MSI as anchor):** The case is as clean as the public record gets. Stated goal (G: well-being, meaningful interaction) and selection pressure (I: engagement, up-weighted reshares and comments) demonstrably diverged; the documented outcome — anger up, feeds saturating with polarizing content within weeks, fixes shelved — followed I. Four project-blind coders converged unanimously, high confidence, on "more-with-I." If H3 gets an anchor, this is it.

**Luhmann (strongest NO — drafted before any consensus forms):** The very evidence that makes the case strong destroys its fit to H3's mechanism. H3 claims outcomes follow pressure *by selection, not by a designer's plan*. But the Facebook Files show leadership *seeing* the harm and *choosing* to reject the fixes because they "might cause fewer users to engage." That is not blind selection operating over a designer's back; it is a designer, fully informed, ratifying the pressure. The case confirms the *outcome* clause of H3 while contradicting its *mechanism* clause. An anchor case must exemplify the causal story, not merely the correlation — and MSI arguably exemplifies the rival story (agency, cynically exercised) better than B's.

**Round 2 — positions.**

**Kant:** I side with Luhmann, on measurement grounds: if the stated G was public relations rather than intent, there was never a genuine G/I divergence — the case then fails §4.3's admission condition, not just its interpretation. The proxy for intent (a Zuckerberg post) is not the thing.

**Meadows:** Partial dissent from the NO. Selection and design are not exclusive: the ecology *selected which fixes could survive internal review*. The shelving of the anti-divisiveness work is selection acting through managers, exactly as variation-and-retention theories predict. The objection narrows H3; it does not evict the case.

**Round 3 — the summoned adversary.**

**Chair summons Le Guin against the emerging "keep it, but reframe" drift:** You are all missing the sampling objection. We know MSI's outcome *because a whistleblower leaked internal research*. Cases enter the documentary record conditional on scandal; de-optimizations that quietly stuck generate no Facebook Files. Anchoring H3 on MSI anchors it on the most outcome-selected observation available — survivorship bias wearing the costume of evidence quality. With n=2 and Case B under-determined, the anchor carries the whole edifice, and it is the least randomly sampled datum in the study.

**Round 4 — chair synthesis (non-voting).**

Agreement-strength: **majority, not consensus.** The panel converges on this as the strongest objection: **the mechanism mismatch — the leaked record shows informed leadership choice, i.e., design ratifying pressure, so the case supports H3's outcome-prediction while undermining its "selection-not-design" causal claim; an anchor that proves the correlation by exhibiting the rival mechanism is a defective anchor.** Kant's PR-reading (no genuine divergence, so no admissible case) is treated as a variant of the same objection. Le Guin's leak-conditioned survivorship objection is ranked second — serious, but it attacks the *pool*, and the pre-registration's widening move already partially answers it; the mechanism mismatch attacks the *case itself* and no protocol amendment cures it.

**Preserved dissent:** Meadows maintains that selection-through-managers dissolves the mismatch and that the objection merely forces H3 into a sharper, multi-level formulation — a refinement, not a defeat. Turchin notes the unanimous blind cross-read should not be discounted by any of this. Both dissents stand in the record.
