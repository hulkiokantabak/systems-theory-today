The question splits into two claims that are usually run together, and they have different epistemic statuses. The first is the taxonomic claim: that the thirteen nodes can be usefully sorted into three tiers. That claim is indeed an organizing convention — no observation contradicts a filing system, any more than an observation contradicts alphabetical order. The second is the structural claim implicit in the arrows: that causal influence runs dominantly downward, from drivers (D1–D4) through dynamics (Y1–Y4) to symptoms (S1–S4), with M as the integrating outcome. That claim has empirical content, and it is falsifiable — provided the project holds itself to three disciplines that keep it from collapsing back into convention.

First, the layering entails mediation. If the wealth pump (D3) produces populism (S3) *through* institutional decay (Y3) and coordination failure (Y2), then conditioning on the dynamics layer should substantially attenuate the driver–symptom association. A robust direct path from a driver to a symptom that bypasses every dynamic — say, optimization pressure (D2) predicting anomie (S4) with the Y-layer statistically held fixed — is evidence against the architecture, not merely an occasion to redraw an arrow. Mediation analysis across countries and decades is hard but not impossible; the claim risks something.

Second, the layering entails asymmetry of intervention and timing. Driver shifts should lead dynamic shifts, which should lead symptom shifts, at least in dominant tendency; and interventions aimed at symptoms alone should show recurrence while the drivers persist. Both are checkable. If populist mobilization were shown to reorganize the wealth pump more powerfully than the pump generates populism — if the "symptom" were the stronger cause — the tier assignment would be empirically wrong. Notably, S2 (attention economy) is the layering's most exposed node: a respectable case exists that it functions as an instrument of D2 and D4 rather than a downstream expression, and the schema should treat that as a live test, not a labeling choice.

Third, and this is where honesty is required: the framework already concedes feedback. Epistemic breakdown (Y1) plausibly amplifies machine-intelligence deployment incentives (D4); fertility collapse (S1) feeds back into optimization pressure. So the falsifiable version cannot be "no upward causation" — that is already false and known to be false. The defensible claim is quantitative dominance: downward effect sizes exceed upward ones over the relevant horizon. That reformulation is weaker but genuinely testable, and it is the version Theory A's adaptation-gap formalism (G = R_c − R_a) actually needs, since the gap is defined by driver-side acceleration outpacing symptom-side adaptation.

The degenerate failure mode is not in the layering but in its maintenance. If every counterexample is absorbed by reclassifying the offending node — promoting a symptom to a dynamic whenever it misbehaves — then the schema becomes unfalsifiable in Lakatos's sense: not refuted, merely immunized. The safeguard is procedural: fix node assignments ex ante, register which cross-layer paths the theory forbids or expects to be weak, and count every reclassification as a cost against the program rather than a free move.

Conclusion: the three-tier vocabulary is a convention; the three-tier causal ordering is a hypothesis. It forbids identifiable observations — unmediated driver–symptom paths, symptom-led reorganization of drivers, upward effects rivaling downward ones — and therefore passes the falsifiability threshold, conditional on the discipline of not redrawing the map each time the territory objects.
