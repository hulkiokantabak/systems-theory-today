**Round 1 — Steelmen.**

**Meadows (strongest YES — H2 is falsifiable).** Rarity is a power problem, not a falsifiability problem. H2 makes a directional within-unit prediction: after a genuine de-optimization event, pathology P falls; if P is flat or rises across the documented set (chronological-feed mandates, shipped "time-well-spent" objectives), H2 is challenged. The B2 case protocol already admits this outcome explicitly — outcomes tracking stated intent, or de-optimizations that fail to move P, both count against the strong reading. A hypothesis with a specified event class, a signed prediction, and a pre-registered "challenged if" clause is falsifiable in the only sense that matters: it can lose.

**Turchin (strongest NO — H2 is unfalsifiable in practice).** The confound is not noise; it is structural. De-optimization events occur almost exclusively *because* of public-pressure episodes (whistleblower leaks, congressional hearings, regulatory threat). Those episodes independently move every plausible P proxy — self-reported well-being, media coverage of harm, usage patterns — in the same direction H2 predicts. So the observable "P fell after de-optimization" is generated equally well by "scrutiny changed reporting and behavior." With N in the single digits and no event free of the confound, no outcome can discriminate H2 from its rival. A prediction you cannot isolate is not testable, whatever the pre-registration says.

**Round 2 — Positions, dissent drafted first.**

**Dissent (Luhmann, drafted before consensus):** Even the "conditional falsifiability" the majority will reach is too generous. The system that produces de-optimization events is the same system that produces P-measurement; observer and observed are coupled. He predicts the panel will mistake procedural rigor (pre-registration, coding protocols) for epistemic access it does not have.

**Meadows:** Falsifiable-in-principle, testable-now via asymmetric predictions: H2 says P stays down after scrutiny fades; the confound account says P rebounds when attention moves on. Time-decay of the pressure episode gives leverage rarity does not remove.

**Turchin:** Concedes the decay design is clever but notes it requires longitudinal P measurement that mostly does not exist retroactively.

**Round 3 — Summoned adversary.**

**Ibn Khaldun (against the emerging "yes, conditionally"):** You are all assuming de-optimization events are exogenous shocks. They are not — platforms *choose* which changes to ship and which to publicize, selecting precisely the changes least likely to hurt engagement (the TTM-low cases). So the observable event set is pre-filtered toward cases where H2 looks good cheaply. This is selection into the sample, worse than confounding: it biases even the decay-timing test, because publicized reversals are timed to scrutiny cycles. Any H2 test must model the event-generation process, not just the event outcomes — otherwise "falsifiable" means "falsifiable against a curated shadow of reality."

**Chair synthesis (non-voting).** Agreement-strength: **majority, not consensus.** The majority position (Meadows, Turchin partially conceding): H2 is falsifiable **in principle and conditionally in practice** — the signed prediction plus two discriminating designs (post-scrutiny persistence of P; engagement-neutral vs. engagement-conflicting event stratification, echoing the TTM split already in the Study-B protocol) can produce outcomes that damage H2. Rarity degrades power, not falsifiability. Conditions: (1) P measured independently of scrutiny-driven reporting, (2) the event-generation/selection process explicitly modeled per Ibn Khaldun, (3) failure to meet (1)–(2) demotes H2 from "tested" to "screened." **Preserved dissent (Luhmann):** the conditions are unlikely ever to be met with observational data, so H2's falsifiability remains nominal until a prospective or platform-cooperative design exists. Ibn Khaldun's selection objection is logged as a binding design constraint, not resolved.
