The strongest objection is that the exemption removes from testing precisely the half of Theory C that does the theory's real work — and does so in a way the project itself profits from.

Recall what C is for. The master puzzle is the loss of shared meaning, with anomie (S4) as its most direct symptom. C's distinctive promise is not merely that a plural, dissent-preserving commons catches more errors than a lone synthesizer; Theory A's adaptation gap and Theory B's optimization ecology already offer epistemic machinery. C earns its seat at the table by claiming something further: that sense-making together is itself meaning-bearing — that the practice of distributed coherence is a partial answer to M, not just a better error filter. Strip that claim out and C collapses into a modest methodological hypothesis about review processes, competitive with neither A nor B in explanatory scope. The meaning-half is therefore not an optional annex; it is the load-bearing wall.

Leaving that wall outside measurement creates three compounding problems.

First, asymmetric falsifiability. The ablation can falsify the epistemic half either way — same catch rate, or provenance collapsing onto one voice. But the meaning-half can never lose. Whatever the ablation shows, the deeper claim survives untouched, which means C as a whole can only be partially refuted while remaining fully assertable. A theory whose decisive claim is structurally immune to evidence is not being protected from category error; it is being protected from risk.

Second, credibility transfer. Suppose the ON arm wins decisively. The report will say, carefully, that only the epistemic claim was tested. But theories do not travel with their caveats. A confirmed C-epistemic will be read — by the project, by future forks, by the author's own motivated cognition — as evidence for C entire. The measurable half generates prestige that quietly capitalizes the unmeasured half. This is the exact mechanism the project's own capture warnings identify, and it is sharpened here because C is the theory asserting that a project exactly like this one is the answer. The self-serving structure demands a higher evidentiary bar, yet the exemption sets a lower one: the flattering half is tested, the grandiose half is excused.

Third, the unmeasurability claim is itself too strong. "A method is not a meaning" is a sound conceptual cut, but it does not follow that the meaning-half has no observable consequences. If participating in distributed sense-making is genuinely meaning-bearing, participants should show it: voluntary return without extrinsic incentive, retention through conflict rather than exit, self-reported purpose, reduced anomie markers relative to matched non-participants. These are proxies, imperfect and gameable — but the project accepts severity-weighted catch taxonomies and entangled baselines as imperfect too, and mitigates with pre-registration and honest caveats. Declaring the meaning-half unmeasurable in principle, rather than hard to measure in practice, converts a research frontier into a sanctuary.

The synthesis is not that the current boundary is dishonest — it is stated with unusual candor — but that candor about an exemption does not neutralize the exemption's effects. The honest resolution is to reclassify: the meaning-half is not outside measurement but awaiting operationalization, with named candidate proxies, a commitment that persistent failure to find any observable trace counts against it, and an explicit firewall preventing epistemic results from being cited on the meaning-half's behalf. A hope that can never be disappointed is not a hypothesis; C should not be allowed to keep one.
