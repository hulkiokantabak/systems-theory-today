As stated, the master-condition claim is not yet falsifiable — but it is falsifiable in principle, and the distance between those two conditions is precisely measurable. The obstacle is twofold. First, the framework deliberately leaves M's position open (the base-or-summit question, Q-002): M-as-base and M-as-summit make opposite causal claims, and a hypothesis that reserves the right to be either cannot be refuted by any observation, since evidence against one reading confirms the other. Second, M is defined — loss of shared meaning, belief, and a common frame — in terms that overlap the operational content of the symptom clusters, especially anomie (S4) and epistemic breakdown (Y1). If every instrument that measures M also measures S4, the claim that M causes the symptoms is unfalsifiable by construction: it is the symptoms, renamed.

The redescription worry is therefore not rhetorical but psychometric. The claim becomes testable only after two commitments the framework has so far withheld. (1) Commit to a directional reading — or test both as rival models rather than one dashed ambiguity. M-as-base predicts that coherence loss temporally precedes symptom onset and moderates the driver-to-symptom pathways; M-as-summit predicts M is a lagging aggregate with no independent downstream force. These are distinguishable, so the ambiguity is resolvable, not fatal. (2) Operationalize M with discriminant validity: an index of shared-frame density (overlap in trusted sources, participation in common ritual and narrative, agreement on procedural legitimacy) constructed to correlate imperfectly — measurably below unity — with anomie scales, populism vote share, fertility intentions, and attention-economy exposure. If no such index can be built, that is itself a finding: M fails as a construct and the redescription charge stands. Falsifiability begins at the measurement model.

Given those commitments, the load-bearing claim yields three concrete tests. The strongest is a moderation-by-coherence natural experiment: identify populations exposed to comparable driver intensity — acceleration, optimization pressure, wealth-pump extraction, algorithmic media — but differing in antecedent coherence (cohesive religious communities, strong-identity ethnolinguistic enclaves, high-trust Nordic municipalities versus matched low-coherence populations). If M is causally load-bearing, high-coherence populations should show attenuated symptom expression at equal driver dose; if M is redescription, symptom levels should track drivers regardless, and "coherence" should simply co-move with the symptoms themselves. Second, a lead-lag panel test: in longitudinal cross-national data, does the coherence index Granger-precede symptom shifts (supporting M-as-base), follow them (M-as-summit), or move contemporaneously with no incremental predictive power (redescription)? Third, a mediation-ablation test that mirrors the project's own graph logic: fit the driver-to-symptom pathways with and without M as mediator or moderator. If deleting M from the model costs nothing in explained variance, M is doing no causal work — the vacuum is a name, not a node.

The claim is falsified if, across these designs, the coherence index shows no discriminant validity, no temporal precedence in either direction beyond symptom co-movement, and no moderation of driver effects. It is corroborated — in the base reading — if antecedent coherence robustly buffers symptom expression under matched driver load. What cannot survive is the current dashed-bidirectional formulation: honest as a record of unresolved dissent, it functions as an epistemic shield if carried into the testing phase. The right conclusion is that M is a testable hypothesis wearing an untestable formulation, and the framework's obligation is to shed the formulation — split Q-002 into two rival, signed models and let the panel's disagreement become the experiment's design.
