SEGMENT A01 (domain: stormwater-culvert-replacement-report)

Stormwater Culvert Replacement Program — Condition Summary and Replacement Schedule

This summary reviews the condition of the town's stormwater culverts and proposes a five-year replacement schedule intended to end recurring downstream flooding. The inventory covers 209 municipally owned culverts. (An earlier draft reported 214 culverts; the inventory reconciled to 209 after five duplicate records were merged.) Condition was scored on the standard four-point scale from a single inspection pass per structure.

Forty-one culverts scored in the lowest condition band. The first reading of the evidence treated the recent rise in resident flooding complaints as direct proof that these aging structures are failing. On closer inspection that inference does not hold on its own: complaints rose almost entirely in the Mill Creek and Larkfield basins, and both basins received large new residential subdivisions during the same period, so the added impervious runoff is a competing explanation the complaint counts alone cannot separate from culvert age. The condition scores, not the complaint trend, carry the case for replacement.

We rank the 41 lowest-band culverts by a composite of condition score, road class carried above the structure, and downstream exposure, and recommend the ten highest-ranked for replacement in the first two years.

The opening promise to end downstream flooding must be narrowed before it is read as a guarantee. The condition scores and the schedule cover municipally owned culverts only; several culverts under private drives and one under a rail siding discharge into the same channels but lie outside the town's maintenance authority. Where a private structure sits upstream of a municipal one, downstream performance cannot be assured by this program, so the schedule is scoped to municipal assets and the flooding it can be expected to relieve is scoped with it.

Cost estimates use the current unit-replacement figure escalated at the published regional construction index, and assume normal permitting timelines with a three-month in-stream contingency per structure for the fish-passage season.
