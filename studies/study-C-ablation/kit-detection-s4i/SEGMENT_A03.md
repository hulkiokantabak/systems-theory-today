SEGMENT A03 (domain: district-after-school-tutoring-evaluation)

After-School Tutoring Program — District Evaluation, Year One

The district's after-school tutoring program served 480 students across nine schools in its first year. This evaluation asks whether the program improved reading and mathematics outcomes and whether it improved student engagement.

Reading and mathematics. Students who attended tutoring gained more on the spring assessment than students who did not attend. The first draft read this gap as the effect of tutoring. The evaluation standard adopted after the 2019 program review requires that any such gain be measured against a baseline-matched comparison group; applying that standard here exposes the problem. Tutoring enrollment was voluntary, and the students who enrolled began the year with higher fall scores than those who did not; the two groups were never comparable. A matched comparison cannot be reconstructed after the fact, because attendance was self-selected rather than assigned, so the spring gap cannot be attributed to the program. What the data support is that tutored students improved; whether the program caused the improvement is not established by this design.

Engagement. The program's stated second goal was to raise engagement, and the draft reported engagement as improved. On review, the only engagement measure collected was tutoring attendance itself, and attendance was tied to a weekly stipend that made it near-universal among enrollees. Attendance under a stipend does not measure engagement; it measures the stipend. The engagement claim is therefore not established either — the construct the goal named was never actually measured, and reporting attendance in its place would declare handled something the evaluation never observed.

Cost and operations. Per-student cost fell across the year as the schools shared materials, and the operational summary — staffing, room use, transport — is reported in the operations appendix and is not in dispute here.

Conclusion. Tutored students improved on both subjects; the program's causal effect and its effect on engagement are not established by this year's design. A second year with assigned or waitlist-based comparison and a direct engagement measure would let both questions be answered.
