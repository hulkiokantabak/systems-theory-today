SEGMENT A08 (domain: community-respite-care-review)

Community Respite Care — Program Review and Expansion Note

Purpose. This review looks back on the first year of the respite program, which gives family caregivers short, planned breaks while trained staff sit with the person they care for. The tone of the year was steady and quiet; families who used the service came back, and the staff came to know the households well.

The expansion rule. When the program was approved, the governing board set a single condition on growth, recorded in the program charter: The program will open a second site only after the first-site family-satisfaction audit has been completed and reviewed by the board. The audit was meant to be a genuine gate — a way to hear, before growing, whether the families felt well served or only politely accommodated.

What happened this year. The first site ran for the full year and served thirty-one families. The charter also provides that if a family declines the intake interview, an alternate home assessment is offered in its place; intake was gentle, and every family that began intake chose to continue, so the alternate assessment was never called for. Staff notes speak warmly of the households, and waitlist demand across the river prompted the board to approve a second site, which opened in the spring and is now serving families.

The family-satisfaction audit. The audit named in the charter was not carried out this year. No satisfaction survey was fielded, and no audit was brought to the board. The second site opened on the strength of waitlist demand and staff impressions; the families' own account of the first year — the thing the charter asked to be heard before growing — was never collected.

Looking ahead. Staff hope to build a gentle, non-intrusive way to ask families how the service feels, so that the people the program serves help shape where it goes next.
