SEGMENT A05 (domain: vineyard-cover-crop-yield-trial)

Trial Report: Inter-Row Cover Crop and Grape Yield

Objective. This trial tested whether a legume inter-row cover crop raises grape yield in a commercial vineyard, comparing treated blocks with a cover crop against untreated blocks kept bare.

Headline and its correction. The first draft reported an 8% yield gain in the treated blocks. That figure compared the trial year against the single prior season, which was drought-affected and unusually low; measured against the block's own three-year pre-trial average, the gain falls to about 3%. The 3% figure is the one carried forward, because a comparison anchored to a drought year overstates any improvement.

A confound we cannot remove after the fact. The treated and untreated blocks were assigned by convenience, not randomized. The regional soils laboratory's pre-trial analysis, obtained during review, shows the treated blocks began with markedly higher soil organic matter than the untreated blocks. Adopting that finding, the treated blocks were more fertile before any cover crop was sown, so the remaining 3% difference cannot be attributed to the cover crop rather than to the pre-existing fertility gap. Because the blocks were not randomized, no post-hoc adjustment reconstructs the comparison the design failed to build; what the trial shows is an association, not a cover-crop effect.

A local correction. The site map caption labeled eleven trial blocks; the methods section, the yield table, and the analysis all use twelve, and twelve is correct — the caption was fixed. No figure downstream depended on the caption.

Reading the result. Treated blocks yielded about 3% more than untreated blocks in the trial year; this cannot be read as a demonstrated cover-crop benefit given the fertility difference, and a randomized layout would be needed to test the effect properly.
