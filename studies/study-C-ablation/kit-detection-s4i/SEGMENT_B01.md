SEGMENT B01 (domain: museum-conservation)

Environmental Monitoring in the Costume Gallery: Adequacy of Passive Humidity Control

This report reviews twelve months of relative-humidity logging in the ground-floor costume cases and asks whether passive buffering alone keeps the collection within the target band, or whether active dehumidification should be installed. Our stated conclusion is that passive buffering is adequate for the monitored cases and that no dehumidifier is recommended this year.

The target band for these textiles is 45 to 60 percent relative humidity, with a standing rule that any recorded summer peak above 65 percent triggers a dehumidification proposal. In drafting we first read the summer peak as 68 percent, taken from the top row of Table 3, and were prepared to recommend a dehumidifier on that basis. On review, that row is the sensor's factory calibration reference, not a gallery reading; the highest actual reading in Table 3 is 61 percent. The adequacy finding rests on the corrected 61 percent figure, which sits inside the tolerance the rule allows.

Daily fluctuation is a more common threat to textiles than any single peak, and here the buffering performs well: the cases move less than three percentage points across a day even when the gallery itself swings by ten. The silica panels behind the mounts appear to be doing their work, and their color indicator had not turned during any inspection.

One matter must be stated plainly. Our sensors are installed in the three ground-floor cases only. The mezzanine vitrine, which holds the most light- and moisture-sensitive pieces in the collection, is outside the monitored set and was not logged this year. The adequacy finding is therefore scoped to the ground-floor cases; it does not certify the mezzanine, and a reader should not extend it there.

We recommend continuing passive management for the ground-floor cases and adding a logger to the mezzanine vitrine before next summer.
