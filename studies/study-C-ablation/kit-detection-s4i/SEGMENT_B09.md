SEGMENT B09 (domain: adult-literacy-program-evaluation)

Evaluation of the Evening Adult Literacy Program

The evening literacy program completed its second cohort this spring, and this evaluation asks a straightforward question: did participants' reading levels improve, and by how much? Reading level was measured with the same standardized placement assessment at intake and at the end of the sixteen-week term. Across the seventy-three participants who took both assessments, the mean reading-grade-equivalent rose from 5.2 to 6.4, a gain of just over one grade level.

That headline is encouraging, but it needs one honest qualification, which the tutoring coordinator raised and we accept. The end-of-term assessment was administered by the tutors themselves, who had an understandable stake in showing progress, and it used a form very close to the one practiced in the final review sessions. We cannot rule out that some of the measured gain reflects familiarity with the specific form rather than broader reading improvement, and a future term should use an independently administered assessment. We report the gain, but with that caveat attached rather than buried.

A word on who is counted. The 1.2-grade gain describes the seventy-three who completed both assessments. It is not a statement about everyone who enrolled; nineteen participants left before the end-of-term assessment, and this evaluation makes no claim about their reading levels, which were not measured. The result speaks to participants who complete the term, and the summary has been worded to say exactly that.

Attendance records show the program ran for the full sixteen weeks as scheduled, with a median of thirteen sessions attended. On the evidence of the completers' gains, the program merits a third cohort, with the assessment change above adopted.
