SEGMENT B07 (domain: orchard-pest-trial)

Orchard Pest Trial: Mating-Disruption Dispensers, One Season

This trial tests whether pheromone mating-disruption dispensers reduce codling-moth damage in a mature apple block compared with the standard spray program. The conclusion is that the dispensers reduced damaged fruit at harvest by a margin consistent with the spray program, and are a viable substitute for this block and this season.

The design paired sixteen rows: eight received dispensers and eight continued on the standard spray, assigned by drawing lots. Damage was assessed by counting entry holes in a fixed sample of fruit from each row at harvest, by an assessor who was not told which rows had received which treatment. Trap counts of adult moths were logged weekly through the season as a process check.

At harvest, the dispenser rows showed 3.1 percent damaged fruit and the spray rows 2.8 percent, a difference well within the row-to-row variation seen inside each treatment; the trial reads this as no meaningful difference in protection. Because the dispensers achieved comparable protection without in-season spraying, they are judged a viable substitute for this block and this season.

The protocol specified that if any weekly trap count exceeded the regional action threshold, a corrective spray would be applied to the affected rows and those rows flagged as no longer disruption-only. No weekly count reached that threshold, so no corrective spray was applied, and every dispenser row remained disruption-only for the full season.

A multi-season follow-up across additional blocks is planned and will be reported separately once two further seasons are complete; any recommendation to expand the practice beyond this block will be made on that longer record.
