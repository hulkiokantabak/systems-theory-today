SEGMENT B03 (domain: transit-safety)

Platform-Gap Incident Review: Suburban Line Stations

This review examines reported boarding-and-alighting incidents at the twelve suburban-line platforms over three years and asks whether the recently widened boarding thresholds have reduced falls into the platform gap. The conclusion offered here is that the widened thresholds are associated with fewer gap incidents and should be extended to the remaining platforms.

The opening summary framed the stakes in stark terms, stating that gap incidents 'have historically caused thousands of catastrophic injuries every year on this line.' That figure is not one this review can support; the incident log records forty-one gap-related injuries across the whole three-year window, of which three were serious. We correct the summary sentence: the safety case for the thresholds does not rest on the inflated total and stands on the actual forty-one-incident record, which still shows a clear decline after the retrofit.

A more consequential point concerns the comparison itself. The incident rate is reported per station, but four of the twelve platforms had their service hours cut midway through the study, so the same raw count spans far fewer boardings there. Left uncorrected, a fall in raw incidents at those four stations would be credited to the thresholds when it partly reflects fewer passengers exposed. We have recomputed the affected stations on incidents per hundred-thousand boardings; on that basis the decline is smaller than the raw counts suggested but remains present, and the recommendation holds on the exposure-adjusted figures.

We recommend extending the widened thresholds, with incident tracking continued on the per-boarding basis rather than the per-station raw count.
