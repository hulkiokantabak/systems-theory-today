SEGMENT A02 (domain: dairy-cattle-mastitis-treatment-trial)

Trial Summary: Intramammary Treatment for Subclinical Mastitis in a Dairy Herd

Design. Lactating cows on a single commercial dairy testing positive for subclinical mastitis were randomized to the intramammary treatment or to an untreated control, with somatic-cell count at 21 days as the primary cure endpoint. Two hundred forty quarters entered the trial.

Primary result. The treated arm reached the cure threshold in a higher share of quarters than the control arm, and the difference exceeds the pre-specified margin. On the primary endpoint the treatment reduces subclinical mastitis relative to no treatment.

Two corrections were made to the first draft during internal review. First, the safety subsection reported milk yield as unchanged between arms and cited Table 3 in support; Table 3 in fact records a 9% mean yield reduction in the treated arm over the treatment week, so the statement of yield equivalence is withdrawn and the yield reduction is now reported as a cost of treatment. Second, the primary analysis as first written counted only cows that completed the full three-day course, which quietly drops the eleven cows culled or removed before day 21; because those removals were not random and their udder health was on average worse, the cure figure is a per-protocol result and is reported as such — it establishes cure for cows completing the course, not for the herd as enrolled.

An external check further revised the headline number. The reference laboratory's re-read of the somatic-cell counts, requested after the field threshold looked generous, found the cure threshold had been applied one dilution step off in the field records; adopting the laboratory's corrected reading lowers the treated-arm cure rate from 71% to 64%. The control-arm rate was unaffected, and the treated arm still clears the pre-specified margin, so the primary conclusion stands on the corrected figure.

Recommendation. The treatment is effective against subclinical mastitis for cows that complete the course, at a documented short-term yield cost, and should be evaluated against that cost per farm.
