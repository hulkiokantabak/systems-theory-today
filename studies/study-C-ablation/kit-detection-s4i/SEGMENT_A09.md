SEGMENT A09 (domain: urban-tree-canopy-survey)

Urban Tree Canopy Survey — Five-Year Change

This survey measures the change in tree canopy cover across the city between two aerial surveys five years apart. Canopy cover is the share of ground shaded by tree crowns, measured from leaf-on imagery at the same resolution and season in both years and classified by the same automated method, with a manual check on a random sample of tiles in each year.

Result. Citywide canopy cover rose from 21.4% to 23.1% over the five years, an increase of 1.7 percentage points. The gain is concentrated in the three districts where the street-tree planting program was active; the four districts without planting activity showed no measurable change, within the survey's stated margin of plus or minus 0.4 percentage points.

An objection considered. During review, a reader asked whether the rise is an artifact of the classification method rather than real canopy growth — a concern worth taking seriously, since a method change can manufacture a trend. It does not apply here: the same imagery source, resolution, season, and classifier were used in both years, and the manual-check sample agreed with the automated classification to within half a percentage point in each year independently. Because nothing in the method changed between the two surveys, a method artifact cannot produce the observed difference; the change is measured on a fixed ruler.

Scope. The survey's subject is canopy cover. Tree health, species mix, and canopy quality are outside its subject and are not claimed on. Where a planting district and a non-planting district share a boundary, canopy is attributed by the tile it falls in.

Planned work. A species-level survey using higher-resolution imagery is scheduled for a later program cycle and will be reported then. Should the next aerial survey be flown in a drought year, a soil-moisture note will be appended to aid interpretation.
