# RESPONSE 12

The strongest objection is that "holding" is doing illegitimate work in the claim: distribution dissolves the very property that makes a theory total. Call it the integration objection.

A theory is not a heap of true sentences; it is total in virtue of its inferential integration — the fact that its parts constrain one another, that a change in one region propagates obligations everywhere else. For that constraint to be real rather than notional, someone or something must actually traverse the connections: check that the account of institutional decay is consistent with the account of attention capture, that the wealth-pump mechanism and the coordination-failure mechanism do not quietly assume incompatible models of the agent. If no single mind holds the whole, then "the commons holds it" reduces to two weaker facts — the parts are severally held, and an index to them is shared. But integration is precisely what cannot be sharded. Coherence-checking between distant regions of the theory requires co-presence in one working intelligence; a commons whose members each hold a fragment performs only pairwise, local checks along existing seams. Global consistency is never verified by anyone, and errors that live between subcommunities — in the joints where one group's simplification is another's load-bearing premise — are invisible in principle. What the commons holds is a library. A library can be plural, living, and even self-correcting at the level of individual volumes while the collection as a whole asserts contradictions.

Worse, plurality actively protects those contradictions. Self-correction fires only when a contradiction is detected, and detection requires holding both conflicting parts at once — exactly the capacity the distributed arrangement forgoes. So the commons' celebrated virtues, forkability and dissent-preservation, become mechanisms for keeping incompatible commitments alive indefinitely under the honorific "productive disagreement." The theory never fails as a whole because it never exists as a whole to fail.

Two replies suggest themselves, and their weakness is instructive. First: mature science already works this way — no physicist holds all of physics, yet physics coheres. But physics coheres through crisp formal interfaces (conserved quantities, dimensional analysis, renormalization conditions) that let local workers trust distant results without inspecting them. A total theory of the present civilizational condition has no such interfaces; its central terms — legitimacy, meaning, attention, decay — are interpretive, and the validity of a seam between two subaccounts is a matter of judgment, not proof-checking. Interface contracts cannot be written where the vocabulary itself is contested. Second: machine intelligence could traverse the whole inferential graph, verifying globally what no human can. But this reply concedes the objection rather than answering it — the integrated theory is then held by a single (artificial) mind, reinstating exactly the concentration of epistemic authority the commons was designed to avoid, and doing so in an artifact whose own reliability the commons cannot audit, for the same reason it needed the artifact.

The objection is strong precisely because it does not attack the commons' sociology — capture, drift, gatekeeping, the ordinary failure modes any institutional design might mitigate. It attacks the logical grammar of the claim. A commons can hold many theories, host their competition, and preserve their disagreements; what it cannot do, by the nature of integration, is hold the one theory that totalizes them. The honest conclusion is not that the commons is worthless, but that its product should be renamed: not a total theory held collectively, but a maintained ecology of partial theories — which is a different, humbler, and perhaps more defensible ambition.
