# RESPONSE 11

**Round 1 — Steelmen.**

**SEVERIN (for the objection, YES).** The strongest objection is underdetermination-by-design. Thirteen nodes admit 78 possible signed edges; the evidence base — mostly observational, mostly last-fifty-years, mostly WEIRD — cannot discriminate among the vast family of signed digraphs consistent with it. Worse, thirteen sits at the granularity where the graph is too large to test edge-by-edge yet too coarse to specify mechanism: "Y3 institutional decay" bundles regulatory capture, elite overproduction, and legitimacy loss, which have different signs toward S3 populism. The set is also suspiciously symmetric — one meta-node, four drivers, four dynamics, four symptoms — a 1-4-4-4 lattice that smells of aesthetic carving, not empirical joints. A taxonomy this tidy will absorb any observation somewhere, which is the classic failure mode of grand social theory: unfalsifiable by architecture.

**IONA (against the objection, NO).** Every node count faces the same charge. Five nodes would be untestably vague ("modernity causes bad things"); forty would be an unestimable mess. Thirteen was chosen so each pressure-test in the thirteen-test battery lands on a distinct node — the vocabulary is indexed to the evidence protocol, not to numerology. And the signed-graph form is precisely what makes ablation studies like this one possible: you can delete a node and ask what explanatory work is lost. A vocabulary you can ablate is not unfalsifiable.

**Round 2 — Positions, dissent first.**

**Dissent (SEVERIN, drafted before any consensus):** Even if thirteen is workable, the project risks premature lock-in. Theories A, B, and C are supposed to compete, but all three are now expressed *in* the thirteen-node vocabulary; the frame has become the referee of its own contest. The adaptation-gap G, the H1–H3 hypotheses, and distributed coherence should each have been allowed to demand their own node set, with translation costs made explicit.

**Emerging position (IONA + chair-adjacent voices):** A shared vocabulary is the price of comparability; the objection is real but manageable via periodic node-audit and the ablation program itself.

**Round 3 — The sharper adversary.**

**KASPAR (summoned against the emerging agreement).** You are all arguing about *count* when the objection is about *type*. A signed causal graph presumes the system decomposes into stable variables with monotone pairwise influences. But the project's own candidate theories deny this: optimization ecology (B) says the units are selection processes, not variables; distributed coherence (C) says the relevant object is a field property, not a node. If the theories are right, the thirteen-node graph is not coarse — it is category-mismatched, and no resizing fixes that. The "audit and ablate" remedy tests robustness *within* the formalism and cannot detect the formalism's own inadequacy.

**Round 4 — Chair synthesis (non-voting).**

Agreement-strength: **majority, not consensus.** Majority view: the strongest objection is Kaspar's type-objection — the signed-node ontology may misrepresent selection-dynamic and field-like phenomena that Theories B and C posit, and internal ablation cannot surface this. Severin's underdetermination and symmetry worries are absorbed as corollaries: they explain *why* the mismatch stays invisible. Iona's dissent is preserved in full: the vocabulary is a working scaffold indexed to the test battery, and demanding formalism-neutrality before evidence-gathering is a recipe for never starting. Recommended discipline: retain thirteen nodes, but add one standing check per study asking what the result would look like if the true ontology were processes or fields rather than nodes.
