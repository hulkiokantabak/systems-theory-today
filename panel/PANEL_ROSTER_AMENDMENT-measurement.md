# PANEL ROSTER AMENDMENT — a measurement / causal-inference seat

Version: 1.0 · Status: **RATIFIED (author, Session 4e); applied to `panel/PANEL_ROSTER.md`.** · Last updated: Session 4e (placed by Code)
Amends: `panel/PANEL_ROSTER.md` (the Advisory Twenty → Twenty-two). Prompted by the recommended catch in `studies/study-B-optimization/PRE_REGISTRATION.md` §10 (logged as C-014).

> **The gap.** The panel was composed for *philosophical* deliberation — the nature, history, and meaning of total systems. The move into **empirical studies** (A, B, C) exposed a missing competence: **causal identification, construct validity, and quasi-experimental design**. Turchin partly covers it (falsifiability, retrodiction), but on the mechanics of *"is this construct valid and is this effect identified from this data,"* the panel had to summon a voice ad hoc during the Study-B deliberation. This amendment makes that competence standing — and, in the project's own spirit, adds it as **two voices chosen to disagree with each other**, not one house methodologist.

---

## Proposed additions to the Advisory Twenty

**21. Donald T. Campbell** (1916–1996) — *the conscience of measurement and quasi-experiment.*
- **Brings:** the honest toolkit for social questions that **cannot** be randomized — **quasi-experimental design** (interrupted time series, regression discontinuity, the named catalogue of *threats to validity*); **construct validity** (does your measure measure the thing, or a convenient shadow of it?); and **Campbell's Law** — *"the more any quantitative social indicator is used for social decision-making, the more subject it will be to corruption pressures"* — which is, uncannily, **Theory B's mechanism stated as a law of measurement.** Also evolutionary epistemology: knowledge grows by blind variation and selective retention (a deep cousin of the project's catches→learnings loop).
- **Key works:** *Experimental and Quasi-Experimental Designs for Research* (with Stanley); "Reforms as Experiments"; "Assessing the Impact of Planned Social Change" (Campbell's Law).
- **Picks fights with:** **Pearl** (your formal DAGs assume you already know the structure; real social data is a minefield of confounds you *haven't* modeled — name the threats to validity before you write the do-operator); **Turchin** (retrodiction is good, but construct validity comes first — are "elite overproduction" and "the gap" *measured* or *reified*?); and **the project itself** (are O, P, G, and "a catch" valid constructs, or are you counting shadows and calling it evidence?). Campbell is the panel's demand that a number *mean* something before it is trusted.

**22. Judea Pearl** (b. 1936) — *the formalizer of causation.*
- **Brings:** the machinery to say **what causal claim is identifiable from what data** — the ladder of causation (association → intervention → counterfactual), directed acyclic graphs, the do-calculus, confounder/collider logic. Directly relevant to the **pressure-test causal hypothesis**, which *is* a DAG: Pearl turns "draw the arrows" into "state which arrows are identifiable, and what would confound each."
- **Key works:** *Causality*; *The Book of Why* (with Mackenzie).
- **Picks fights with:** **Campbell** (your "threats to validity" are ad hoc where a formal model would be systematic — a DAG tells you *exactly* which confounds matter and which are irrelevant, no catalogue needed); **correlational social science** ("no causes in, no causes out" — a regression is not a cause); and the project's **arrows** (you drew a causal graph — now commit to the identification claims, or admit the arrows are associational). Pearl is the panel's demand that causal language be *earned* by a stated identification strategy.

## Why two, and why these two

Campbell and Pearl **disagree productively** — the pragmatist of imperfect data against the formalist of causal structure — which is exactly the designed-disagreement pattern the panel runs on. On any empirical study the project attempts, Campbell will ask *"is the construct valid and what are the threats?"* and Pearl will ask *"what is your identification strategy and DAG?"*, and the tension between those two questions is where good empirical design lives. Neither alone would have caught the full set of problems in the Study-B deliberation; together they cover the competence the philosophical roster lacked.

## Scope of the amendment

- These are **advisors** (on call), not core members — the core ten remain the philosophical/historical/systems spine.
- Summon them for: any study's pre-registration; any causal claim in the pressure-test graph; any construct-validity question about O, P, G, or the catch taxonomy; and any place a number is about to be trusted.
- Like all panelists, they are **reconstructed positions, not the persons** (Ground Rule 16).

## Recommended catch (logged as C-014)

Log as a catch: *"the panel was composed for philosophical deliberation and lacked a measurement/causal-inference competence; the empirical turn (studies A/B/C) exposed the gap, which was first patched ad hoc in the Study-B deliberation and is now proposed as a standing amendment (Campbell + Pearl)."* Class: process. Consequence: this amendment; a standing practice of summoning the measurement seat whenever a study or a causal claim is on the table.
